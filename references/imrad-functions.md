# IMRaD Functions

Use this reference when the scientific meaning is stable enough to organize a paper or section.

## Draft in Functional Order

Start from the central question and evidence chain, not polished sentences. A practical order is Results -> Methods -> Discussion -> Introduction -> Abstract -> Title, adapted to available material.

## Section Contracts

### Introduction

Move through research territory, specific known state, unresolved gap, why it matters, objective or hypothesis, and bounded approach. Do not write a broad textbook review.

### Methods

Cover materials or participants, design and controls, sequence and conditions, measurements, replication and exclusions, analysis, and ethics or registration when relevant. Do not insert methods not supplied or verified.

### Results

Organize by research question or evidence block. State the local comparison, anchor it to a figure or table, report the decisive pattern with units and uncertainty, include supplied statistics, and end with a bounded result-level takeaway. Keep unsupported causes out.

### Discussion

Move through principal finding, proportional interpretation, comparison with checked work, alternatives, limitations, justified implications, and the next discriminating test. Do not convert “consistent with” into “demonstrates.”

### Abstract and Title

Write after the central claim and evidence chain are stable unless explicitly provisional. Do not claim mechanism, causality, scope, or generality beyond the evidence.

## Paragraph Contract

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Function | The job this paragraph performs |
| Evidence anchor | Figure, table, method, or checked source |
| Key message | One sentence the reader should retain |
| Known-to-new flow | Context preceding the new information |
| Transition | Why the next paragraph follows |

## Admission to Scientific English

Advance when intended meaning, evidence status, terminology, and paragraph function are explicit enough that editing cannot accidentally change the science.
